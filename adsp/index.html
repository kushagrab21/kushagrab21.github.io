What these numbers can tell you

All scores are out of 100. The overall average uses all 520 students. Six have no attendance recorded: their scores count in that overall average, but they cannot appear in the attendance chart or a group selected by attendance.

A difference between the groups does not tell us whether attendance caused the difference in scores.

What I built

From a spreadsheet
to an answer.

I built ADSP’s analysis and assistant workflow.

Give ADSP your data and ask a question in ordinary language. It chooses an analysis, runs the calculation and explains the result. The answer includes references to the data it used, so you can inspect what it is based on.

The number comes before the explanation.

ADSP calculates a result, then uses AI to describe it. Keeping the calculation separate from the writing means there is a computed result to check against the explanation.

You can try a small part of that here.

This example uses one preset question and fictional student records. Move the attendance slider: the selected students turn blue in both the sheet and the chart, and their scores make up the average. Click a dot to read one student’s row. Keep a comparison to hold one answer while you explore another group.
